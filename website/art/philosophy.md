# Conductive Bloom

*An algorithmic philosophy for WattWatch*

Conductive Bloom treats electricity as a growing organism rather than a line on a chart. Charge leaves the wall the way water leaves a spring. It pours from two narrow slits, braids itself into channels, and climbs toward a single luminous body that it slowly fills. Nothing in the image is drawn directly. Every filament, every knot of brightness and every wisp of heat is what's left behind by thousands of particles each obeying the same small set of forces. Like a load meter, the picture can only be read over time. A single frame shows a little current; a thousand frames show where the energy went.

The flow is a negotiation between three fields. A potential well draws every particle toward the orb, strengthening as it nears, so paths that begin as chaotic tendrils converge into dense, inevitable arteries. A precomputed curl field built from layered noise introduces turbulence that is divergence-free: it bends and folds the current without ever thickening or thinning it, the way a real conductor routes flow around obstacles without losing charge. Near the orb a gentle tangential swirl turns arrival into orbit, so energy spirals inward instead of striking head-on. The balance among these forces is the craft of the piece. It should read as the product of deep computational expertise, with every coefficient tuned until the braids look grown rather than plotted.

Alternation runs underneath everything. Each particle carries a phase that advances at mains frequency, and that phase pushes it sideways, perpendicular to its direction of travel. The result is a fine, regular serpentine woven into every strand. Far from the orb the alternation is broad and visible. Close in it collapses into a shimmer, the way an alternating supply becomes a steady glow once it's rectified and smoothed. Switching between fifty and sixty cycles changes the stitch length of the whole composition. That small detail is felt more than seen, and it's the kind of painstaking calibration a master of the form would refuse to skip.

How much current flows is set by the load, and the load is never linear. The emission rate follows a concave power curve, so a lightly loaded system already sends a surprising stream of charge. Not all of that charge arrives. At birth each particle is quietly assigned a fate by an efficiency curve that sags at low load and plateaus near the middle. The unlucky fraction decouples partway along its journey and becomes heat. It turns from cool current into magenta embers, loses its attraction to the orb, and rises on buoyant turbulence until it fades. The waste is never labelled. You see it as smoke peeling away from the main flow, more of it when the system idles, less of it when the system works.

The orb itself is an integral. It has no brightness of its own. Each particle it absorbs adds a quantum of energy, and its glow follows a saturating curve of that running sum, like a meter that only ever counts upward. Sparks flare where charge lands, and the accumulated trails turn white-hot where paths overlap most densely. Seeded randomness keeps every run reproducible. The same seed always grows the same bloom, and neighbouring seeds grow its cousins. Each run proceeds until the source is exhausted and the last ember has cooled, leaving a still image that records its whole history. The final artwork should look as if it took countless iterations to perfect: a meticulously crafted algorithm, refined until order emerges from the chaos with complete confidence.
