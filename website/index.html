Honest about accuracy

Software can't see your wall socket. So here's what it does.

WattWatch always tells you which kind of number you're looking at: an estimate, a calibrated estimate, or a real measurement.

  1. Read real sensors

    CPU package power from Intel/AMD RAPL on Linux, powermetrics on macOS or LibreHardwareMonitor on Windows. GPU power from NVIDIA NVML or the amdgpu driver.

  2. Model the rest

    Memory, SSDs and hard drives, fans and the motherboard are estimated from load and disk activity. CPU TDP comes from your exact model.

  3. Add PSU losses

    DC load becomes wall draw through an 80 PLUS efficiency curve. At low load a big PSU can waste a fifth of everything.

  4. Calibrate

    Type in a reading from a plug-in meter like a Kill-A-Watt. Two readings (idle and busy) fit a straight-line correction to your machine.

  5. Or just measure

    Point it at a Shelly, Tasmota or TP-Link Kasa plug on your network and the wall number becomes a true measurement.

Pcpu = Pidle + (Pmax − Pidle) × (2u − u1.4) · u = CPU utilisation (Fan, Weber & Barroso, 2007)

Install WattWatch

You need Python 3.10 or newer. WattWatch opens its dashboard in your browser at http://127.0.0.1:8787 and adds an icon to your system tray.

Just want to look around?
Run python -m wattwatch --demo for a throwaway database with three weeks of sample history.

          
Start with your computer
Turn on “Start WattWatch when I log in” in Settings → System. It can only count what it sees while it's running.

          
Real CPU watts on Linux
RAPL counters are root-only on many distros. The README shows a one-line rule that allows reading them.

        

      

    

  


  
  

    

      
Questions
FAQ

      

        
Is WattWatch free?
Yes. It's open source under the MIT license. There's nothing to buy, no premium tier and no sign-up.

        
Does it send my data anywhere?
No. The server listens only on 127.0.0.1, history lives in a SQLite file in your user folder, and smart plugs are read directly over your LAN. No analytics, no cloud.

        
How accurate is it without a meter?
Typically within about 30% of the wall reading. Machines with an NVIDIA GPU and RAPL access land closer because the two biggest consumers are measured. One Kill-A-Watt reading usually gets you within 10%.

        
Which smart plugs work?
Shelly Gen1 and Plus/Gen2+ plugs, anything running Tasmota with energy monitoring, and TP-Link Kasa HS110, KP115 and HS300. Newer Kasa firmware needs pip install python-kasa.

        
What about laptops?
On battery, WattWatch counts 0 W from the wall because the energy is billed later, when you charge. While plugged in it adds an estimate for charging, and uses a laptop-brick efficiency curve.

        
Does it count time while the PC is asleep?
Yes. It notices sleep and shutdown gaps and books them at your sleep or standby draw (2 W and 0.5 W by default), which is also how sleep streaks are tracked.

        
Will “Apply power plan” mess with my settings?
It only sets two values for when you're plugged in: display off after 10 minutes and sleep after 30. It asks first, and you can change both back in your OS power settings.

        
Can I change the electricity price later?
Yes, and with time-of-use periods too. Energy already recorded keeps the price it was tracked at, so past totals don't jump around.
